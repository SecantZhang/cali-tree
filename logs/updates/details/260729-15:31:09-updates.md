## Motivation

The one-call, two-cutpoint architecture is preferable to Cali-Tree routing, but partial
performance remains weak. After scalar aggregation failed, the next question was whether a
semantically structured lower boundary could help—or whether the v4 score representation
itself had already discarded the required evidence.

## Design decisions

The selected progress definition was the minimum of change evidence and specification
fidelity. It produced no change on EditInspector and reduced ImagenHub accuracy by 5.00
points, balanced accuracy by 1.94 points, and partial F1 by 7.75 points. It was rejected
without a runtime implementation.

The collision audit found only 25 distinct score triples across 392 EditInspector cases and
39 across 600 ImagenHub cases. Respectively 95.83% and 94.83% of partial cases share their
entire triple with at least one no/yes case. An accuracy-maximizing in-sample tuple lookup
recovers only 29.17% and 17.24% of partial cases. Sixteen of 23 score triples shared across
datasets have different modal labels.

Therefore v6 changes the information emitted by the rubric, not downstream architecture.
It remains one judge call and two global cutpoints, with no routing, model ensemble,
optimizer, or learned feature classifier. Its condition ledger and residual taxonomy were
derived from the full 392-case calibration evidence, so those cases are not represented as
independent validation. Only the untouched 391-case final partition can confirm it.
