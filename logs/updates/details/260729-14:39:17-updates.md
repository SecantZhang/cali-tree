# 260729-14:39:17 — Expose partial-label reliability and reject soft calibration

## Motivation

The simple Rubric-Lite candidate improves the EditInspector partial boundary, but its
remaining partial F1 could reflect both judge mistakes and disagreement among the three
human ratings. The next iteration needed to quantify that distinction and test whether a
small probabilistic calibrator could improve partial without recreating Cali-Tree's
over-engineering.

## Changes

- `vejudge/interface/node_calibration/calitree_nodes.py`: added deterministic mapping of
  individual SC ratings and a `human_label_reliability` report with per-target unanimity,
  target-majority support, rating entropy, modal-rater agreement ceiling, and expected
  prediction-to-rater agreement.
- `web/src/panels/RightPanel/secondary/CaliTreeWorkbench.tsx`: added a compact human-label

- `tests/unit/interface/node_calibration/test_calitree_nodes.py` and

  calculations and rendering.
- `docs/calitree.md`: recorded the EditInspector and ImagenHub rater-agreement audit and
  the leakage-safe nested comparison of two cutpoints against soft-label multinomial
  calibration.

## Design decisions

The runtime remains one global v4 rubric call followed by two scalar cutpoints. On the 392
EditInspector calibration cases, only 8.33% of the 24 partial targets are unanimous and
their modal-rater agreement ceiling is 61.11%. The two-cutpoint model has 58.33% partial F1
on disputed items.

A fully nested five-by-four-fold audit selected model family, regularization, class
weights, and probability threshold only inside each outer training fold. Soft-rater
multinomial calibration reached 85.46% accuracy but only 58.28% balanced accuracy and
28.57% partial F1; partial-F1-directed selection reached 32.73% partial F1. Both are below
the two-cutpoint model's 68.03% balanced accuracy and 35.44% partial F1. The probabilistic
model was therefore rejected rather than added as another runtime branch.

The modal-rater metric is explicitly named an agreement ceiling, not an accuracy ceiling:
the released median-derived target remains the primary benchmark label.
