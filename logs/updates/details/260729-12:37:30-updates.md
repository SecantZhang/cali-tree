# 260729-12:37:30 — Promote simple Rubric-Lite partial-progress verification

## Motivation

The complete Cali-Tree hierarchy is expensive and difficult to justify for deployment, while its full-coverage partial F1 remains low. Rubric-Lite v4 showed that one global three-score rubric could approach the tree's accuracy, but a broad second-pass v1 verifier reduced confirmation accuracy. This update tests whether a narrowly learned semantic boundary can improve partial cases without adding routing, embeddings, optimizer calls, editor features, or per-task prompts.

## Design decisions

The primary judge remains Rubric-Lite v4: one global call emits change evidence, specification fidelity, and source preservation; two global training-fitted cutpoints map the minimum score to `no`, `partial`, or `yes`. The verifier uses a separate general rubric whose deterministic rule is `no` for zero requested progress, `yes` for exact fulfillment of every requested condition, and `partial` for recognizable but incomplete progress.

Twenty-seven global fusion policies were compared on 232 official training cases and a task-disjoint 120-case development slice. The selected policy retains primary `no` and `partial` results and rejudges only primary `yes` results. It uses no editor, task UID, instruction cluster, or held-out label at inference.

The broad v1 verifier was rejected: on the frozen 600-case confirmation it changed accuracy from 82.17% to 79.67% and partial F1 from 29.51% to 29.33%. The selected partial-v2 policy used 51 verifier calls and changed accuracy to 82.33%, partial recall to 34.48%, and partial F1 to 31.01%. This is 0.50 points below complete Cali-Tree v2's 82.83% full-coverage accuracy with a much smaller architecture.

The gain is not statistically established. A paired 10,000-repeat task bootstrap gives an accuracy-delta 95% interval of -0.83 to +1.17 points and a partial-F1-delta interval of -1.77 to +6.02 points. Nine predictions changed, four became correct, and three regressed; exact McNemar p=1.0. Cross-dataset generalization remains untested.
