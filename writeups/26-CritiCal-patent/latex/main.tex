\title{\critical: Calibrating Multimodal Judges for AI-Assisted Video Editing Evaluation}
% \subtitle{Technical report \\ Draft date: November~5, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
When multimodal language models judge AI-edited videos, their raw verdicts often disagree with humans: a single judge can miss a brief visual defect, favor surface polish over instruction compliance, or flip its answer when candidate order is swapped. We introduce \textbf{\critical}, a framework that calibrates multimodal video judges without retraining or fine-tuning their underlying models. \textbf{\critical} combines complementary video and frame-level judges with structured adversarial debate to surface and verify the visual factors that drive human preference. A lightweight calibration model and reliability gate then fuse these signals into a human-aligned preference score while automatically deferring ambiguous comparisons for closer review. Across video-editing benchmarks, \textbf{\critical} reaches up to \textbf{85.40\%} agreement with human evaluators at full coverage (up from \textbf{75.96\%} for a single baseline judge) and \textbf{93.40\%} on accepted cases when deferring the most uncertain half of comparisons.
\end{abstract}
