\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multimodal language models are increasingly deployed as automated judges in content-creation and quality-control pipelines. In AI-assisted video editing, an editing system receives source assets and a user instruction and produces one or more candidate edited videos. A judge evaluates whether the outputs satisfy the instruction, preserve relevant content, and form a coherent audiovisual experience. However, even when a model writes a fluent explanation, its verdict often disagrees with human evaluators---for example, it may prefer a visually smooth clip that misses a requested step, get fooled by an on-screen text label that does not match the actual video, or change its answer simply because the two candidate videos are presented in reverse order.

Existing methods attempt to fix judge errors through evaluator fine-tuning~\cite{prometheusvision,videoscore}, prompt and rubric engineering~\cite{geval,autocalibrate}, or post-hoc score calibration~\cite{quantllmj,mtbench}. Yet none of these can recover visual evidence that a single judge failed to see, resolve trade-offs between competing editing criteria, or tell when a model's verdict is too unstable to trust. Closing this gap requires looking at the video through multiple lenses, actively testing competing claims against the footage~\cite{chateval}, and knowing when to defer to a human.

\paragraph{Our approach.}
We introduce \critical, an evidence-grounded calibration framework that turns noisy multimodal judgments into reproducible, human-aligned preference decisions without retraining the underlying models. Given two candidate video edits, \critical pairs native-video and frame-level perceivers in counterbalanced display orders with an adversarial debate-and-referee stage that verifies contested claims directly against the footage. A lightweight statistical model and a reliability gate then fuse these signals into a calibrated preference probability and route each comparison to automatic acceptance, bounded follow-up inspection, or human review.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Counterbalanced multi-perceiver judging:} We combine complementary native-video and frame-based judges across swapped display orders to separate genuine indifference from position bias and track modality missingness explicitly.
  \item \textbf{Media-grounded adversarial factor discovery:} We pair opposing advocate agents with an independent referee that verifies claims against the video footage and distills contested points into structured, reusable factors.
  \item \textbf{Calibrated fusion and selective reliability routing:} We fuse perceiver scores, stability diagnostics, and verified factors into calibrated preference probabilities and decouple preference prediction from decision reliability---reaching \textbf{85.40\%} full-coverage human agreement on VE-Bench and \textbf{93.40\%} at 50\% selective coverage.
\end{itemize}
