\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Evaluator training and rubric prompting.}
Prior work improves automated judges by fine-tuning vision-language evaluators on rubric-scored datasets, as in \textit{Prometheus-Vision}~\cite{prometheusvision} and \textit{VideoScore}~\cite{videoscore}, or by structuring, optimizing, and hierarchically merging evaluation prompts, as in \textit{G-Eval}~\cite{geval}, \textit{AutoCalibrate}~\cite{autocalibrate}, \textit{HarmonicEval}~\cite{harmoniceval}, TextGrad~\cite{textgrad}, GEPA~\cite{gepa}, and \textit{Cali-Tree}~\cite{calitree}. Unlike evaluator fine-tuning or single-judge prompt optimization, \critical keeps foundation models frozen and combines complementary video and contact-sheet representations to overcome single-perceiver blind spots.

\paragraph{Post-hoc calibration, multi-agent debate, and selective prediction.}
\textit{MT-Bench}~\cite{mtbench} documents position and verbosity biases in LLM judges, while \textit{Quantitative LLM Judges}~\cite{quantllmj} fits post-hoc regression models from a judge's score and rationale to human feedback. \textit{ChatEval}~\cite{chateval} introduces multi-agent debate among text evaluators, and selective prediction methods~\cite{geifman2017selective,madras2018predict,kamath2020selective} trade coverage for accuracy by abstaining or deferring to humans under high uncertainty. \critical unifies and extends these directions for video editing: it pairs counterbalanced multi-perceiver scoring with media-grounded adversarial debate, fuses the resulting features into a calibrated preference probability, and uses a diagnostic reliability gate for selective prediction.
