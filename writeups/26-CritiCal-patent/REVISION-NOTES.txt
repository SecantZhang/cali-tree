Substantive review and revisions
1. Evaluation protocol conflation: separated the nominal 972-pair presentation selective curve from the 986-pair saved fusion result. The 85.1% and 85.40% endpoints are different protocols. The separate baseline experiment (83.02% versus 76.14% at 80% coverage) is explained in prose, not overlaid as a control for the presentation curve.
2. Component attribution: disclosed 972 frame judgments, 24 debate records, and zero graph coverage out of 986 comparisons. The fusion gain does not isolate debate or graph effects or establish superiority over every standalone frame judge.
3. Statistical head ambiguity: distinguished the candidate-oriented vector psi in the no-intercept Bayesian head from the full router vector phi. Explained swap antisymmetry, Gaussian prior MAP fitting, vote weights, local covariance approximation, clipping, and frozen normalization/missingness conventions. Clarified that the logistic expert router has different labels, inputs, and symmetry properties.
4. Validation leakage: expanded grouped fitting and out-of-fold evaluation; normalization, category selection, and routing thresholds stay within training groups. A supervised risk head must use training-side held-out verdicts, not evaluation-fold correctness.
5. Missing evidence and order stability: missing debate is masked, allowing direct measurements to continue; an order-consistent 0.5 is uncertainty rather than proof of genuine indifference or correctness.
6. Reliability policy: clarified that investigation updates both fusion and risk; automatic acceptance still requires the acceptance threshold. Otherwise the budget bounds further acquisition, with explicit failure/fallback/deferral behavior.
7. Selective evaluation: added weighted coverage and accepted-case accuracy definitions, distinguished pair coverage, and described noncommittal predictions. Deferred pairs are not counted as successful human resolutions; observed operating points do not prove monotonicity or complete-system efficiency.
8. Worked example: restored its slide-reported status; no claim that the stated probability change was independently reconstructed or caused by one identified coefficient.
9. Probability quality: preserved AssemblyBench log-loss and Brier improvements, while noting the reported accuracy increase is not established as nonzero by its grouped-bootstrap comparison.
10. Reproducibility: specified folds, seeds, cohorts, feature coverage, and group resampling where documented. Exact model/prompt versions, pairing rules, routing thresholds, and costs still require the archived run manifest. The paper names these requirements without inventing settings.
