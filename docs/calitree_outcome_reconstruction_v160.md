## What the strict rubric gets wrong

All four Sol human-Yes mistakes report `all_material_facets=yes`: upright bottle,
potato placed on board, player catching ball, blue shoe changed to white running
shoe. The old tree downgrades them for changed bottle appearance, removed peeler,
missing glove/extra balls, or a changed second shoe. The model's explicit outcome
observations can be right while a universal fidelity cap misaligns the grade.

Preservation still matters in some fitting cases. Pancakes appear while other
foods/cookware are replaced; a door opens while the foreground child is replaced
with other people. Humans assign Partial. These are larger semantic changes than
minor accessory or appearance differences, but the current single preservation
category does not distinguish them.

## Executable outcome-primary policy, v160

The new shared rubric uses four branches:

1. No newly realized requested content, without a contradictory complete claim:
   No.
2. Newly realized content plus all requested outcome facets and scope complete:
   Yes.
3. Newly realized content plus an established incomplete requested outcome:
   Partial.
4. Unknown/contradictory outcome evidence: defer.

Source/operation fidelity remain observations and potential selective guard
features; they are not universal caps. The v150 policy and all observations stay
immutable. This is a new decision policy, with its own contract hash.
