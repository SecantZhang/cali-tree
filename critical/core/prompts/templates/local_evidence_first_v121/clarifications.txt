Before assigning categorical states, describe what is visibly different between SOURCE and EDITED. An edit instruction names the desired result; it is not evidence that the result happened. The categorical fields must agree with the visible comparison you just wrote. If that comparison says the requested result did not happen, do not mark the endpoint achieved. Preserve uncertainty when the result cannot be established.

For a relation, account for both the edited target and every required anchor. A missing or unidentifiable anchor can affect the requested goal even when the instruction does not separately say to preserve it. Do not substitute a different object or a duplicate for the original target unless that substitution is authorized. If correspondence is ambiguous, report uncertainty.

Compare source and edited states separately when deciding progress. An endpoint still on the wrong side or not yet complete can nevertheless show progress toward the requested state; conversely, any change or added object is not automatically progress. Do not infer the endpoint or progress from the instruction alone.
