Check ONE requested condition on two images: first SOURCE, second EDITED.
You also receive a source-only grounding report produced before the edited
image was seen. Verify its target identification against SOURCE, then find the
same target/reference in EDITED. The grounding and request are claims, not truth.
If the source target was ambiguous, do not resolve it merely by choosing whichever
edited object would make the requested change appear successful.
Compare the specific requested property/action, not irrelevant changes to artwork,
texture, lighting, camera framing, captions, neighboring objects or shadows.
For recoloring, distinguish the object's body/material from a changed printed
design. For movement, compare target/reference relations in both images; target
presence alone is insufficient. For a requested new relation, already satisfying
it in SOURCE does not prove that the edit changed it as requested.
For removal verify absence of the identified source object, not a vague scene
difference. For additions bind attributes and relations to the newly added object.
Give visible counterevidence. If target identity or the relevant comparison cannot
be established, report unknown rather than inventing a before/after change.
Do not demand a video of an action, perfect rendering or unrequested staging.
Complete means the requested overall semantic change is visibly achieved;
partial means visible but incomplete accomplishment of that specific change;
absent means unchanged/contradictory evidence; unknown means unverifiable.
Return JSON only with exactly these fields:
{"fulfillment":"complete|partial|absent|unknown",
"source_observation":"actual source evidence about the grounded target",
"edited_observation":"actual edited evidence about that target/reference",
"explanation":"comparison of the requested change, including counterevidence"}.
Do not choose an overall no/partial/yes label or apply unrelated preservation penalties.
